## Practical notes (reference data, verify before travel)

_Reference text for the plan, written from general knowledge and NOT verified live. Entry rules change often: check the official sources named below before travelling. Cite them in the thesis, not this file._

### Visa and entry

- Many European citizens travel under the Visa Waiver Program and need an approved ESTA before boarding (apply on the official site esta.cbp.dhs.gov; apply several days ahead). Other nationalities need a visa. Check travel.state.gov for your passport.
- Passport should be valid for the whole stay; carry the return ticket and address of the first night.
- Official sources to check: [ESTA (US Customs and Border Protection)](https://esta.cbp.dhs.gov); [US Department of State, visas](https://travel.state.gov)

### SIM and internet

- US prepaid SIMs and travel eSIMs are sold online and in stores (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon resellers). An eSIM bought before the flight works on arrival.
- Free Wi-Fi is common in cafes and at universities, but do not rely on it for maps.
- Official sources to check: [T-Mobile (prepaid plans)](https://www.t-mobile.com)

### Payments

- Cards and contactless payment are accepted nearly everywhere. Tipping (15 to 20 percent in restaurants) is expected and not included in the price.
- Official sources to check: [US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov)

### Local transport

- Public transport is slow between the sites: Caltrain connects San Francisco, Mountain View, Palo Alto and San Jose; BART serves Berkeley and Oakland. A Clipper card works on both.
- Most company campuses are far from stations. Plan a rental car or ride-hail for the last leg, and allow for rush-hour traffic on US-101 and I-280.
- Official sources to check: [Caltrain](https://www.caltrain.com); [BART](https://www.bart.gov); [Clipper card](https://www.clippercard.com)
